Discrete-time optimal control: main ingredients (I)
The main ingredients of a discrete-time optimal control problem are
Dynamics: a (discrete-time) system in state space form
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut)
t = 0, . . . , T −1
We will mainly work on a finite horizon, i.e. 0 < T < +∞(notation: 0, . . . , T −1). In some cases we
will also consider an infinite horizon, i.e., T = +∞.
The dynamics introduces T equality constraints
x1 = f(x0, u0)
i.e.
ft(x0, u0) −x1 = 0
x2 = f(x1, u1)
i.e.
ft(x1, u1) −x2 = 0
...
...
xT = f(xT −1, uT −1)
i.e.
ft(xT −1, uT −1) −xT = 0
